Case Study · Rescuing the River Lea from the Edge Ethnographic Research Proposal
01
Research Question

Rescuing the River Lea
from the Edge

What are the key challenges and opportunities in the service experience of houseboat residents on River Lea?
  • The River Lea has long been labelled London's "edgeland" — post-industrial, overlooked.
  • Yet it has always been a space for social experimentation and alternative living.
  • Houseboat residents are the most contemporary expression of that spirit — and the most invisible to city services.
02
Wider Phenomenon

Alternative urban living & the limits of service design

  • Rising housing costs are pushing people toward non-conventional living arrangements.
  • Existing service systems are built on the assumption of a fixed address — systematically excluding mobile residents.
  • Who does the city design for? Who gets left out?
Theoretical lens — Doreen Massey: place identity is dynamic, shaped by multiple competing narratives.
03
Methodology & Method

M1Methodology — Ethnography

  • Focuses on lived experience in real-world contexts.
  • Service experience cannot be quantified — it must be understood from the inside.
  • McGranahan (2018): "the importance of an ethnographic sensibility."

M2Method — Cultural Probes / Photovoice

  • Participants document daily service experiences through photography over 1–2 weeks.
  • Photos act as prompts for in-depth photo-elicitation interviews.
  • 6–10 residents along the Hackney Wick stretch, varied lengths of residence.
  • Data analysed via Thematic Analysis — Braun & Clarke (2006).
